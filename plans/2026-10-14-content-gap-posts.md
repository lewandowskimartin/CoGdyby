## 1. Power cut compensation: what you're owed and how you get it

- **Slug:** `power-cut-compensation-uk`
- **Title:** Power cut compensation in the UK: what you're owed and how to get it
- **Keywords (to verify):** power cut compensation uk · power cut compensation 12 hours · how much compensation for power cut · storm power cut compensation
- **Intent:** money, after the event. Readers arrive during or just after a storm.
- **Group:** Power cuts and winter
- **Length:** about 900–1,100 words. Short. One table carries most of it.

**Outline**
1. **The short answer.** A table of what you're owed and when (normal weather vs severe weather).
2. **Normal weather.** From Ofgem, checked 14 Oct 2026:
   - **£100** if off for more than **12 hours** (under 5,000 homes affected), or more than **24 hours** (5,000 or more affected).
   - **+£45** for each further 12 hours.
   - Cap **£400** where 5,000 or more homes are affected.
3. **Storms (severe weather).** "Category 1" is 8–13 times the normal faults: **£90** for the first 24 h, then **£45 per extra 6 h**. "Category 2" is 13 times or more: **£90** for the first 48 h, then £45 per 6 h. The cap across both is **£2,235**.
4. **Do I have to claim?** Most payments are **automatic** from your network operator, not your supplier.
   - The exception: **£100 extra if your power went off more than four times in a year (1 April to 31 March), each cut lasting 3+ hours**, which you have to claim.
   - If a payment is missed, chase the operator. A late payment can carry an extra **£40**.
5. **Who to contact: 105**, which reaches your network operator, not your energy supplier. NI: 03457 643643.
6. **Gas cuts too** (short): as read on 14 Oct, homes get £50 for planned cuts, £40 more if the operator didn't give 7 working days' notice, and £70 for each further 24 h of an unplanned cut. Re-check the gas lines on Ofgem before publishing; they're the easiest to misread.
7. **Priority Services Register.** No extra payments, but extra help during a cut. Link the PSR post.
8. **What compensation won't cover.** Spoiled food is usually a home insurance question. Point to "check your policy", with no promises.

- **Sources:** [Ofgem: Check if you can get a payment for a power cut](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/information-consumers/check-if-you-can-get-payment-power-cut), plus your DNO's guaranteed-standards page (e.g. National Grid). Note "2026 adjustments to the Guaranteed Standards".

## 2. Broadband or mobile down: what to do (and how to keep working)

- **Slug:** `broadband-mobile-outage-uk`
- **Title:** Broadband or mobile down? What to do in a UK internet outage
- **Keywords (to verify):** broadband down what to do · internet outage uk · wifi down working from home · mobile signal power cut · does wifi work in a power cut
- **Intent:** practical, in the moment, plus "before next time".
- **Group:** Power cuts and winter (power is the main cause of outages)
- **Length:** about 1,200–1,400 words

**Outline**
1. **First two minutes.** Is it you or everyone? Restart the router once, check your provider's status page on mobile data, check for a power cut (105).
2. **When the power's off too.** The router stops, and a digital landline stops with it. Link the switchover post.
   - **Mobile masts have limited battery backup**, so your signal may fade within the first hour or two of a wide cut.
   - **Verify against Ofcom** before writing: the "about a fifth of sites last an hour" figure and the June 2026 resilience guidance come from a secondary source (Selectra).
   - **999 or 112 works on any network.**
3. **Staying connected.** Phone hotspot (check your data allowance), a 4G/5G backup router for home workers, a power bank sized for phone plus hotspot.
4. **Working from home.** Tell your employer early, know your laptop's battery life, download files you'll need, and use a library or café with power and Wi-Fi. A short "before it happens" list.
5. **What still works offline.** Downloaded maps and playlists, a battery or wind-up radio for local news, cash because card machines may fail, and numbers on paper.
6. **If it lasts days: compensation.** Ofcom's automatic compensation (from 1 Apr 2026): **£10.34 per day** for a delayed repair after loss of service, and **£32.31** for a missed engineer appointment.
   - Verify the trigger (after 2 full working days) and the list of providers signed up on Ofcom's "Automatic compensation: need to know" page.
7. **Who needs to plan harder.** Telecare alarms, anyone who relies on the landline. Link the switchover post and PSR.

- **Sources:** [Ofcom: automatic compensation rises (8 Apr 2026)](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/phones-and-broadband/accessibility/quicker-complaints-resolution-and-more-money-in-your-pocket-when-things-go-wrong), the Ofcom network resilience guidance (find the primary source), and gov.uk Prepare.

## 3. Do you need a portable power station? An honest UK guide

- **Slug:** `portable-power-station-uk`
- **Title:** Do you need a portable power station? An honest guide for UK homes
- **Keywords (to verify):** portable power station uk · power station for power cut · best portable power station for home uk · power station vs generator
- **Intent:** commercial investigation, so this is the affiliate post. The angle that wins trust: **"most homes don't need one"**.
- **Group:** Kit, water and food
- **Length:** about 1,400–1,700 words, with one sizing table

**Outline**
1. **The honest answer first.** For a typical few-hour UK cut, a torch, a power bank and a radio cover it. Who a power station *is* for:
   - medical equipment at home (follow the device maker and your clinician, and join the PSR)
   - home workers
   - rural homes with long cuts
   - anyone on the PSR waiting for help
2. **What it is.** A big rechargeable battery with plug sockets. It isn't a generator: silent, no fumes, safe to use indoors.
3. **Sizing in plain numbers.** A table of watt-hours (Wh) with typical draws: phone charge, laptop, router per hour, LED lamp, CPAP night ("check your device's figure"), kettle (why it's a bad idea).
   - Three sizes: about 250–300 Wh, about 500–700 Wh, about 1,000 Wh+.
   - **Prices only as ranges with a date**, or none. Never "affordable".
4. **What to look for.** LiFePO4 battery (more charge cycles, more stable), UK three-pin outlets, pure sine wave, recharge time, solar input (optional, honest about UK winter sun).
5. **Safety.**
   - Charge on a hard surface, not overnight in an escape route, and use the maker's charger. Cite London Fire Brigade or another fire service's lithium-battery advice; find the primary page.
   - **Never run a petrol generator indoors or in a garage** (carbon monoxide).
   - **A gas boiler can't simply be plugged in.** It needs a qualified electrician to fit a suitable connection. Say "ask a registered electrician", no how-to.
6. **Cheaper alternatives.** A bigger power bank, an LED lantern, a flask, and blankets.
7. **Our picks.** Three tiers through a new Amazon list, plus the honest line: "buy one only if one of the reasons above is you".

- **Sources:** gov.uk Prepare (power cuts), the fire service on lithium batteries, PSR via Ofgem/ENA, maker spec sheets for the Wh figures (cite as "typical").

## 4. Is my food safe after a power cut?

- **Slug:** `food-safe-after-power-cut-uk`
- **Title:** Is my food safe after a power cut? A UK guide to the fridge and freezer
- **Keywords (to verify):** is food safe after power cut · power cut freezer food · can I refreeze after power cut · fridge power cut how long
- **Intent:** **after** the cut. "The power's back; what do I bin?" This is the difference from `what-to-do-during-a-power-cut-uk`, which covers *during*.
- **Group:** Kit, water and food
- **Length:** about 900–1,100 words. Make it the definitive page and point the existing posts' food lines to it.

**Outline**
1. **The 30-second answer.** Fridge about 4 h, full freezer up to 48 h, half-full about 24 h. FSA "estimates", since appliances vary.
2. **The fridge.** The FSA lets food sit under **8°C** during a cut. If it reached 8°C or above, chilled food (meat, fish, ready-to-eat, "keep refrigerated", leftovers) should be eaten within 4 h or thrown away. Use a fridge thermometer.
3. **The freezer.** Full vs half full. Partly thawed food: **check the FSA's refreezing advice** (not on the page fetched 14 Oct). Our existing posts already say the FSA advises against refreezing; confirm the exact wording.
4. **Winter trick.** Below 8°C outside, food can go outdoors in a sealed box, away from sun and animals. FSA.
5. **What's always fine.** Tins and cupboard food.
6. **Extra care for** pregnant women, young children, older relatives and anyone vulnerable. When in doubt, throw it out.
7. **Insurance.** Some home policies cover freezer contents. Check yours.

- **Printable:** a "fridge-door food card" (4 h / 48 h / 24 h / 8°C). Doubles as a Pinterest pin and a possible free download.
- **Sources:** FSA power cut food safety guidance (food.gov.uk). Find the canonical page, not the print version.
