How to use this pack

Twenty minutes at the kitchen table now means nobody's working it out under pressure later. Do it together — children included.

1
Fill it in
Go through each page by hand. Skip anything that doesn't apply.
2
Print it
Any home printer, colour or black and white. Print a spare for the grab bag.
3
Pin it up
On the fridge or inside a cupboard door — somewhere everyone can find it.
999In a real emergency, always call 999 first (or 112). This pack helps you prepare in advance — it doesn't replace the emergency services.
Good to do once a year
Check the numbers still work, update anything that's changed — a new phone, a house move, a new baby — and re-print. Preparedness only works if it's current.

What to do

In an emergency

The official UK advice, and the numbers to have to hand.

If the emergency is outside your home
Go in · Stay in · Tune in
Go in — depending on the emergency you may need to close all windows and doors. Stay in. Tune in — follow national and local news and official advice (local radio and TV, and trusted sources such as your emergency services' social media).
If the emergency is inside your home
Get out · Stay out · Ring 999
Get everyone out of the home, stay out, and ring 999 straight away.

Household Emergency Plan10 / 12
One extra step worth taking

Priority Services Register

Recommended by gov.uk/Prepare and genuinely worth doing: a free service from energy and water companies for anyone who'd need extra help in a power or water cut.

It's worth signing up if anyone in your home:
· is older, or has a disability
· relies on medical equipment that needs power
· has a serious or long-term illness
· has young children
· would struggle to cope in a cut
· needs communications in another format
It's free, and it doesn't affect your bills. To join, contact your electricity, gas or water supplier (each keeps its own register) and ask to be added — or search "Priority Services Register" plus your supplier's name.
